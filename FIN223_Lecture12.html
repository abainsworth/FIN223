Lecture 1: The Investment Management Process

  • Investment philosophy
    1. Statement of beliefs about the markets
    2. Statement of beliefs about the opportunities to create value
    3. Statement of beliefs about the group’s abilities
    4. Statement of beliefs about the group’s ability to exploit the opportunities to create value
  • Investment process considerations
    • Fund objectives
    • Fund constraints
    • Portfolio construction and rebalancing methods

Lecture 2: Investor Decision Making

  • How do we model investor preferences?
  • Utility functions allow investors to rank different investments
  • Risk averse investors require a risk premium to take on risk
  • Remember to ask yourself whether you are receiving adequate return compensation for the risk you are taking
  • Investing over the lifecycle

Lecture 3: Portfolio Theory

  • What investment opportunities are available?
  • Calculating portfolio risk and expected return from individual assets
  • What is the highest expected return the investor can achieve for a given level of risk?
  • Diversification
    • Correlation between asset returns determines the risk reduction
  • Investors select their portfolios by choosing asset weights to maximise utility subject to the available investment opportunities

Lecture 4: Capital Asset Pricing Model

  • Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)
    • Lots of assumptions…
    • Only systematic risk (beta) is priced, in theory
    • Each individual asset’s contribution to systematic risk is what generates returns in this model, in theory
    • All assets lie on the Security Market Line, in theory
  • Estimating the CAPM
  • Measure systematic and diversifiable risk
  • We can re-think how diversification works by focusing on firm-specific (idiosyncratic) risk

Lecture 5: Market Efficiency

  • Do prices reflect all available information?
    • Informational efficiency
    • Market rationality
  • Forms of market efficiency depend on type of information
    • Weak form
    • Semi-strong form
    • Strong form
  • Anomalies
    • A number of well known trading strategies can generate positive returns
    • Are the returns to anomaly strategies compensation for risk?
  • Event studies
    • Use the CAPM to help identify “abnormal” returns
    • Can help explain drivers of stock returns (i.e. information)

Lecture 6: Behavioural Finance

  • What are the behavioural biases that impact investment decision making?
  • Limits to arbitrage
    • Why can’t arbitrageurs take advantage of mispricing?
    • Fundamental risk, transaction costs and model risk

Lecture 7: Responsible Investing

  • What is responsible investing?
  • Why should investors should care about Environmental, Social and Governance factors?
  • What is fiduciary duty and why is it important?
  • Divestment vs engagement
    • How should investors try to change corporate behaviour
  • Do ESG factors impact risk?
  • Is there a performance penalty for being green and responsible?
  • What is “greenwashing”?

Lecture 8: Performance Evaluation

  • There are large number of performance evaluation metrics available
    • Sharpe ratio, Treynor ratio , Jensen’s alpha, etc
  • Measures differ in terms of the risk-adjustment
    • The appropriate measure depends on the risk that you are concerned with (i.e. total risk or systematic risk)
    • They link back to portfolio theory and the CAPM
  • Market timing models allows us to estimate alpha, up-market beta and down-market beta
    • Stock selection and market timing are two sources of investment manager skill
  • Performance attribution allows us to decompose fund performance relative to its benchmark
    • Decompose by industry, country, asset class, etc
    • Two sources of value-add are stock selection and asset allocation

Lecture 9: Bond Pricing

  • Pricing bonds
    • Determine if you receive the next coupon payment
    • The coupon accrues each day (accrued interest)
    • Clean vs dirty prices
  • Why do bond prices change?
    • Change in required yields in the market (i.e. shifts in the term structure)
    • Change in credit quality of issuer
    • Time: bond is approaching maturity
  • Calculation of holding period return
    • Need to consider reinvestment rate and expected sale price
  • Prices and yields have an inverse convex relationship

Lecture 10: Term Structure of Interest Rates

  • It is easy to plot the yield curve
  • We need to extract the term structure of interest rates to price bonds
    • Spot rates are the key numbers we need
    • These are the yield on zero coupon bonds
  • We can bootstrap the term structure from coupon paying bonds if there are no zero coupon bonds
  • Each cash flows has its own discount rate when pricing bonds
  • We can estimate forward rates implied by spot rates
    • They can potentially tells us something about future spot rates
  • Theories of the term structure
    • Expectations theory
    • Liquidity premium theory

Lecture 11: Interest Rate Risk

  • Interest rate risk can be measured using duration
    • Modified or Macaulay
  • Macaulay duration
    • Discounted-cash-flow-weighted time to receipt of all its promised cash flows divided by the price of the security
  • Duration is related to
    • Maturity (+)
    • Coupon (-)
    • YTM (-)
  • Duration allows investors to hedge interest rate risk and/or position their portfolio to take advantage of expected changes in interest rates
